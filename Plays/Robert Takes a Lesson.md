# Robert Takes a Lesson
## Idea Swiped from Walter McCornack.

(Scene—Harvard Stadium. Time—Next September. The Harvard squad is at practice, Prof. Haughton enters, leading little Robert Zuppke.)

PROF. HAUGHTON  
Those are the candidates for my team. What are they?

ROBERT  
The candidates for your team.

PROF. HAUGHTON  
Good. There are 125 of them. How many are there?

ROBERT  
One hundred and twenty-five. Where re the rest of them?

PROF. HAUGHTON  
Probably busy in class. Probably what?

ROBERT  
Busy in class.

PROF. HAUGHTON  
See that yellow oval object the man is kicking? What is it?

ROBERT  
I dunno. What is it?

PROF. HAUGHTON  
That's the football. That other man is now punting it. When he drops the ball from his hands and kicks it before it touches the ground it's a punt. What is it?

ROBERT  
A bunt.

PROF. HAUGHTON  
No, no. Not a bunt. A punt.

---

ROBERT  
What is that squad of men doing at the other end of the field?

PROF. HAUGHTON  
That's a team practicing signals.

ROBERT  
Is that the Harvard team?

PROF. HAUGHTON  
Not Team No. 1. That's Team No. 7, the team that plays Maine, Bates, and Brown. The team practicing in the middle of the field is Team No. 2, that plays Princeton and Cornell. Pretty soon Team No. 2, that plays Yale, will run through its signals.

ROBERT  
Where is Team No. 1?

PROF. HAUGHTON  
I'm giving No. 1 a rest. I don't expect ot have to use it this season. We don't play any western schools.

ROBERT  
Is that man throwing the ball?

PROF. HAUGHTON  
Yes, that's just what he's doing; only we call it passing the ball. He's practicing the forward pass.

ROBERT  
Is that legal?

PROF. HAUGHTON  
O, yes. It's a play eastern teams are using extensively. You'll probably see it in th west in five or six years.

ROBERT  
Who is that man peeking round the corner of the stand?

PROF. HAUGHTON  
Ah! I hand't noticed him. Thanks. (He blows a whistle and an orderly dashes up.) Orderly, there's a newspaper man behind the stand. Summon Companies 4 and 7 of the Cavalry Bouncers and see that he is properly dealt with.

---

ROBERT  
What are those white lines on the ground?

PROF. HAUGHTON  
Those are the ten yard lines, to aid the players and officials in determining how much ground must be gained. What are they?

ROBERT  
The ten yard lines to—to—to—

PROF. HAUGHTON  
To aid—

ROBERT  
To aid the officials—

PROF. HAUGHTON  
The players and officials—

ROBERT  
In determining how much ground must be gained.

PROF. HAUGHTON  
Good. See those upright posts connected by a cross bar at either end of the field?

ROBERT  
Yes.

PROF. HAUGHTON  
Those are the goal posts. When the football is placed, kicked, or drop-kicked over one of those cross bars and between those posts by a Harvard man, it's a goal. What is it?

ROBERT  
A goal. And what is it if the ball is kicked by other than a Harvard man?

PROF. HAUGHTON  
An accident.

ROBERT  
Do accidents often happen?

PROF. HAUGHTON  
Occasionally. But we are rapidly doing away with them.

ROBERT  
How?

PROF. HAUGHTON  
By dropping the offenders from our schedule.

ROBERT  
But surely teams should not be punished for an accident.

PROF. HAUGHTON  
Nevertheless they are. An upstart named Mollwitz or Molebite or something like that accidentally gained two or three hundred yards through the line of Team No. 1½ last fall and the eleven with which he played was dropped like a hot cake.

---

(AN ORDERLY advances.)

ORDERLY  
Your Highness, thirty-six assistant coaches are waiting outside.

PROF. HAUGHTON  
Tell them practice will be over in four minutes.

(THE ORDERLY exits.)

ROBERT  
I wish we could get a game with your Team No. 8 or 9.

PROF. HAUGHTON  
Whom do you mean by "we"?

ROBERT  
Illinois.

PROF. HAUGHTON  
Illinois? Illinois? O, yes, I believe I passed through that place on the way to Chicago. How strong are you?

ROBERT  
We're about the best in the west.

PROF. HAUGHTON  
As strong as Michigan?

ROBERT  
I believe so.

PROF. HAUGHTON  
Do you see that gate marked "Exit"?

ROBERT  
Yes.

PROF. HAUGHTON.  
Sorry you can't stick round a while longer, old top. But I've got to teach Team No. 12 a new play called the crisscross, so that they can work it against Team No. 2. Yale invented the play last week and we must learn to stop it. Of course, I couldn't let you see it.

ROBERT  
I believe I've already seen it.

PROF. HAUGHTON  
Where?

ROBERT  
In high school games out west.

(PROF. HAUGHTON blows his whistle. ROBERT exits in haste.)
