## Characters.
Me and the White Sox.  
Time—Last Sunday afternoon.

## Scene 1.

(Me enters, pages Joe Jenkins and pays him what Me owes him. The game has just started.) 

GLEASON  
Sit down and. rest your dogs.

J. COLLINS  
Big Grover’s pitching. Maybe you'll bring him luck.

ME  
What if Connolly looks over here?

GLEASON  
He can’t see this far.

(Me sits down behind the water cooler.)

WILLIAMS  
Cut ’em through there, Lowdie!

J. COLLINS  
He’s cutting ’em through. He's got something.

GLEASON  
(to Me) You ought to seen me hitting against him in practice the other morning. He threw one and. I ducked me head. and I says to meself, “You’re manager. You don’t have to hit against him.” So I quit.

(Judge of Washington hits a high fly which is caught for the third out. The Sox regulars enter the bench.)

WEAVER  
(picking out his bat) What if a man hit a ball like that and he had a pal in an airplane and the pal ‘swooped down and caught the ball and sailed away with it? What's the rule?

MURPHY  
Buck’s coocooed. He's plain. nuts.

(Me gets up to make room for Lowdermilk and bumps Me head on the concrete roof. Lowdermilk sits down and bumps his head on the concrete roof.).

LOWDERMILK  
This is a sweet bench for birds like us. I don’t know whether to knock my brains out or get stoop-shouldered.

WILLIAMS  
That’s Gallia’s curve ball.

CICOTTE  
That's Shaw pitching.

WILLIAMS  
Who do you think I think it is? But he’s got a curve ball like Gallia’s. It breaks down.

GLEASON  
We’ll break it up before we get through with him.

CICOTTE  
Did you see that in the paper about Gowdy? He’s been fighting in France two years, till he’s so sick of it he’ll never look at the ocean again, and when he came up to bat in Boston yesterday they gave him:a steamer trunk.

J. COLLINS  
It’s a wonder they didn't give him some bully beef.

CICOTTE  
I guess Herzog gets a commission on steamer trunks.
(Between Scenes 1 and 2, Me learns from Mr. McMullin that the Washington catcher’s name is not pronounced Picnic, but as if spelled P. Cynic.)

## Scene 2.
(The Sox regulars come in for their second turn at bat. Mr. Lowdermilk’s engagement ring finger on the right hand is bleeding profusely.)

SCHALK  
How did you do that?

LOWDERMILK  
I do it every time I pitch. I spike it with my thumb nail.

SCHALK  
Doesn’t it hurt?

LOWDERMILK  
Yes, but I’m used to it.

ME  I should think you’d cut down your thumb nail.

LOWDERMILK  
I might do that.

## Scene 3.
(The Sox regulars come in for their third turn at bat. Schalk singles. While Lowdermilk is striking out, Liebold inspects the bats.)

GANDIL  
Use that little cue I got from-Lavan.

LIEBOLD  
I was going to use it.

GANDIL  
It’s like a feather. But if you ever hit against Walter with it there'd be splinters all over the park.

(Lowdermilk finishes striking out and Liebold singles_with Lavan’s recent cue)

GANDIL  
I guess I don’t know how to trade cues. I traded Lavan one that never got a base hit for that one. (Picks up another bat.) And here is a cue I traded Bake’ for. But it’s green.

CICOTTE  
Everything’s green this time of year.

(Weaver gets a-hold of one, drives in Schalk and Liebold and slides safely into third base. With E. Collins at bat Me is informed that “Cocky” will probably try to squeeze Buck home. “Cocky” does try, but misses the ball and Buck is run down on the line after nearly escapnig with a clever fall-away from P. Cynic. Weaver returns to the bench.)

WEAVER  
I near got away from them, didn’t I?

McMULLIN  
I’ll say so.

WEAVER  
And that was a good ball for “Cocky” to bunt, wasn’t it?

McMULLIN  
Yes, it looked like a good ball.

WEAVER  
Well, never mind this time. But what I want now is a new rule. The way it is now a man is entitled to three strikes every time he comes up. I want it fixed so that if a man hits the first ball the way I do the two strikes he’s got left go on his next turn at bat, so the next time he comes up he’s entitled to five strikes.

CICOTTE  
Yes, but with you hitting, you’d have so many strikes coming in that they’d have to play till December to give you your strikes.

MURPHY  
Buck’s just a plain maniac. He’s coocooed.

## Scene 4.
(The Sox regulars come in for their fifth turn at bat. With one out Schalk singles. Lowdermilk, to every one’s amazement, hits safely through short, sending Schalk to third)

WEAVER  
Well, did you see Loozle-Magoozle!

(Schalk scores when Lowdermilk is forced at second and they both come back to the bench.)

SCHALK  
(to Lowdermilk) I guess Faber'll never try to show you how to hit again.

J. COLLINS  
Nice work, Lowdie! And they said Loozle-Magoozle couldn't hit!

LOWDERMILK  
I wore a jewel when I hit that one.

(The side is retired and Leonard, batting for Shaw, walks. Menosky flies out, but Foster triples to right, scoring Leonard.)

GLEASON  
Well ⸻!!!!" (He walks down to the water cooler, presses the faucet, decides he doesn’t want a drink, walks under the stand and back again.)

(Foster scores on Judge's single. Gleason kicks Me in the shin, though Me had nothing to do with it. The next two fly out. The Sox return to the bench.)

GLEASON  
Never mind that, Lowdie. That’s all they’ll get.

SCHALK  
We're still one ahead, Lowdie, and only three innings to go..

LOWDERMILK  
(gloomily) I must have checked my curve ball some place before I came to the park.

## Scene 5.

(A Washington pitcher, removed from action, stops at the Sox bench on his way to thé shower.)

THE W. P.  
What do you think of that old gray haired ⸻? He don’t let any of us finish a game. First thing you know you're out of there. He’s an old gray haired ⸻!

CICOTTE  
Oh, I wouldn't say that about him.

WEAVER  
Well, now, wait a minute. If that’s what he thinks about him, let him say it. Is that what you think about him?

THE W. P.  
That’s what I think about him.

WEAVER  
Well, that’s all right, then, But if you didn’t think it, it wouldn't be right to say it.

## Scene 6.

(The Washington seventh. With a man on second and two out, Thompson is sent up to hit for Ayers. He takes two strikes.)

MURPHY  
Hey! Take that gun off your shoulder and shoot!

McMULLIN  
Hey! What's that you’ve got in your hands? What do you do with that? What did they give it to you for?

LYNN  
You were sent up to get a base on balls, I guess.

CICOTTE  
You’re shoulder’ll get tired with that big heavy cue on it.

(Thompson takes the cue off his shoulder and goes out on a ground ball to Lowdermilx.)

(In the Sox half, Gandil singles and reaches third base on the outs of Risberg and Schalk. Lowdermilk is at bat.)

E. COLLINS  
(to Gandil) Chick. Yes, if you want to.

(Lowdermilk takes a strike.)

E. COLLINS  
(to Gandil) Yes, if you want to, Chick.

(Yes, Chick wants to and is out on a close decision at the plate.)

## Scene 7.

(The Washington eighth. Washington drives Lowdermilk out, pounds Kerr, and is stopped with three runs by virtue of a freak triple play. The Sox come in for their eighth.)

E. COLLINS  
That’s all right, Lowdie. You'll get ’em next game.

LOWDERMILK  
I weakened. And I must have left my curve ball-home. I must have checked it somewhere. But I weakened. But I'll get ’em next time.

(Cicotte exits in the direction of the bullpen. Johnson is pitching for Washington.)

GLEASON  
(to Murphy) Mike, you hit for Dick. Remember, Walter’s just a pitcher. He can be hit.

JACKSON  
We'll hit him.

(Murphy grounds out, but Liebold, with the Lavan cue that would be splintered against Walter, singles to left without splintering the cue. Weaver singles, So does E. Collins. Rice crazily tries to head off Liebold at the plate on Collins’ single and permits “Cocky” to reach second and Weaver third. The Sox are one run behind.)

J. COLLINS  
He’ll walk Joe now.

FELSCH  
If he does, he’ll wish he didn’t.

McMULLIN  
All right, Dill Pickles. You show him.

(Johnson purposely passes Jackson and Felsch breaks up the game with a loud single to left.)

## Scene 8.

(The Washington ninth.)

GLEASON  
(looking anxiously toward the bullpen) Everybody go slow. Eddie ain't ready.

E. COLLINS  
Call in Danforth and then send him back.

(E. Collins unties his shoe-laces and ties them up again. Schalk has trouble with his mask and protector. Gleason pretends not to hear Umpire Connolly’s frantic shouts. Connolly, losing, all patience, rushes at Gleason.)

CONNOLLY  
Come on, now! No more stalling. WHO PITCHES?

GLEASON  
All right, Tommy. It’s Danforth. (To E. Collins) We'll let him pitch to one batter. Eddie ain’t warm yet. .

(Eddie remains unwarm while Danforth pitches to three batters and the game is over. Weaver overtakes Me under the stand and smashes Me in the shoulder blade.)

WEAVER  
Well, old kid, there’s one for the book!

MURPHY  
Never mind him. He's a plain nut and he’s coocooed.
