## Characters
Fred, a Texas steer. (Tenor)  
Gus, A Bum steer. (Barytone)  
Betsey, A cow, wife to Gus. (Contralto)  
Bossy, A cow, sweetheart to Fred. (Soprano)  
Goldstein, An inspector at the Stockyards. (Basso)  
Also steers, cows, horses, prairie dogs, sheep, calves, society men and women, cowboys, a guide, killers at the Yards, etc.  
The opera is in two acts and the action takes place in Texas and the Union Stockyards, Chicago.

## Atto I
Scene—A large ranch in Texas. The scene opens with the stage full of cowboys, cows, and steers.

CHORUS OF COWBOYS.  
> Oh, we're a band of cowboys brave,  
> Yeave-ho, my lads! Yeave-ho!  
> We're here to make the steers behave,  
> Yeave-ho, my lads! Yeave-ho!  
> We're masters of the ranger's art.  
> It's hard to tell us birds apart.  
> We all look just like William Hart,  
> With a hey-nonny-nonny-no!

CHORUS OF STEERS.  
> Oh, here's to the cows that reside on this ranch,  
> Singing Bellow! Singing Bellow!  
> For Bossy and Betsy and Beulah and Blanche,  
> Every fellow give a Bellow!

CHORUS OF COWS.  
> Oh, here's to the steers that reside on this farm,  
> Singing Moo! Singing Moo!  
> Were ever there steers of such exquisite charm?  
> Every Kuh give a Moo!

TUTTI.  
> Oh, what is so gay as this ranch life in Texas,  
> With never a thought of the morrow to vex us!

(TUTTI goes out. BOSSY and FRED enter from opposite directions and meet in the middle of the scene.)

FRED.  
Bossy!

BOSSY.  
'Tis thou!

FRED.  
> Sweetest of Kuhs,  
> I have bad news.  
> Tomorrow I hear  
> Each handsome steer  
> Leaves here for Chi,  
> Alas! to die.

BOSSY.  
Mio Dio!

FRED.  
> Say farewell now,  
> Most comely cow!

BOSSY.  
>    My heart's too full,  
>    You great big bull!

(Fred goes out. Gus, who has been concealed behind a cactus, steps forth, his face livid.)

GUS.  
Thy Frederick will desert thee? (BOSSY shies in alarm.)  
So, Boss! Gus would not hurt thee.  
I only crave thy kisses.  
But hell! Here comes my Mrs.

(Enter BETSY, who observes GUS and BOSSY conversing and rushes at BOSSY threateningly.)

BETSY.  
Ah, now I have thee truly!

BOSSY.  
I fear thee not! thou'rt mooly!

(They are about to mix when a band of strolling prairie dogs enters and BOSSY, BETSY, and GUS go out.)

CHORUS OF PRAIRIE DOGS.  
> Oh, we're a band of prairie dogs,  
> Sing Hickory and Chicory!  
> We love not cattle, sheep or hogs,  
> But only Dame Terpsichore!

(They dance. Enter steers, horses, cowboys, cows, and tutti.)

COWS, COWBOYS, AND HORSES.  
> Oh, what is so gay as this ranch life in Texas,  
> With never a thought of the morrow to vex us!

STEERS.  
Till wealthy Chicagoans come to annex us.

PRAIRIE DOGS.  
Oh, we're a band of prairie dogs.

TUTTI.  
Sing Hickory and Chicory!

PRAIRIE DOGS.  
We love not cattle, sheep, or hogs.

TUTTI.  
But only Dame Terpsichore!

(All are dancing as the curtain falls.)

## Atto II

### Scene 1.

Scene—A courtyard in the stockyards

(Enter a GUIDE with a crowd of society men and women on a sightseeing tour.)

CHORUS OF SOCIETY PEOPLE.  
> O odor enduring!  
>    O perfume divine!  
> Was e'er so alluring  
>    A fragrance as thine!

GUIDE.  
(Pointing.)  
> There is the place where we butcher the hogs  
> And there is the place where we massacre dogs.  
> While this pretty building behind you of course is  
> The place we receive and matriculate horses

CHORUS OF SOCIETY PEOPLE.  
> O odor enduring!  
>    O perfume divine!  
> Was e'er so alluring  
>    A fragrance as thine!

(Enter a band of fatted calves. They dance.)

CHORUS OF SOCIETY LADIES.  
Well this is some show! They don't do things by halves.

CHORUS OF SOCIETY MEN.  
O look at the ballet! O look at the calves!

(At the conclusion of the dance, the guide, the society people, and the calves go out. Fred enters alone.)

FRED.  
> Fair Bossy, how I miss thee!  
> How yearn once more for to kiss thee!  
> But thou art in the Lone Star State,  
> While I await my hideous fate  
> Imprisoned in a slaughterhouse.  

(Bossy enters on the hoof from behind.)

BOSSY.  
My Fred!

FRED.  
How camest thou to Chi? Who letcha?

BOSSY.  
I came on a C. R. I. cowcatcher.

FRED.  
But why camest thou to Chi?

BOSSY.  
That I with thee might die.

FRED.  
> O most courageous Holstein!  
> But hist! Here's Mr. Goldstein!

(Goldstein, an inspector, enters. Bossy hastily disguises herself with a heavy veil.)

---

GOLDSTEIN.  
How now! You steers are loose again?  
Back, cattle, back into your pen!

(Fred, Bossy, and Goldstein go out. A flock of sheep enters. They play games and sing.)

CHORUS OF SHEEP.  
Button, button, who's got the button?  
Soon we'll all be legs of mutton.  
Run, sheep, run! Stop, sheep, stop!  
Soon we'll all be a mutton chop.

(Goldstein enters.)

GOLDSTEIN.  
How now! You sheep are loose again?  
Back, sheep skates, back into your pen!

(Goldstein and the sheep go out. Gus, the bum steer, enters alone.)

GUS.  
My life is saved! O tidings sweet!  
They say I am not fit to eat.  
To Texas I'll return in haste,  
And having slipped the wife a taste  
Of chloroform or cyanide,  
I'll claim proud Bossy as my bride.

### Scene 2.

Scene—Death Chamber at the Stockyards.

CHORUS OF KILLERS.  
O, sweet is our job when we hit 'em just right,  
When the steers go to sleep without saying good night.  
But when we don't bean 'em right square in the forehead  
The way that they holler about it is horrid.

(Forty or fifty steers, heavily veiled, enter the death chamber. Among them are Fred and Bossy. Gus, the exempt, disguised as a lounge, looks on. The Killers raise their tack-hammers and go about their work. First Fred falls, then Bossy. As she strikes the floor, her veil falls off and Gus recognizes her.)

GUS.  
(Anguished.) Bossy! La Bovina! You've killed a milch cow!

(End of the Opera.)
