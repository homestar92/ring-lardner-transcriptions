# A Rotten Deal

## Characters
James Callahan, a Manager.  
Bill Lamewing, a Recruit. 
Joe Patience, his Roommate.

Scene— Paso Robles and other parts of heaven. Time—Any spring.

### Act I
(The first day in camp.)

#### Scene I
(Lamewing's room in the hotel. It is late afternoon. JOE is addressing postcards with a pencil. Enter BILL)

BILL  
Whadd'ya think o' that guy, tryin' to work us to death the first day? He didn't make me kill myself. I ain't no boob and I ain't takin' no chances o' throwin' my arm away. I'll take my time and when I feel like cuttin' loose, I'll cut loose. And then I'll show 'em somethin'. Is this your comb? Let me use it oncet, will ya?

JOE  
I guess that's right.

BILL  
You know it's right. Don't let no manager run ya. First thing you know, your whip'll be gone and they'll be sendin' ya back to the sticks.

JOE  
Sure.

BILL  
If Callahan asks me why I didn't cut loose today, I'll give him a talkin' to that'll hold him awhile.

JOE  
Sure.

#### SCENE II
(The dining room. CALLAHAN and BILL are seated at a table.)

CALLAHAN  
Well, I didn't notice that you hurt yourself today.

BILL  
I was afraid o' workin' my arm too much.

CALLAHAN  
I don't want you to overwork your arm. I meant that you didn't show any life out there.

BILL  
I wasn't feelin' good.

CALLAHAN  
Well, get a good rest tonight and then get to work tomorrow.

BILL  
All right.

### Act II
(The second day.)

#### SCENE I
(The practice field. The players are engaged in batting practice.)

CALLAHAN  
Get out and pitch a few to the batters, Lamewing. You don't need to put anything on it.

BILL  
My arm's pretty sore, Jim.

CALLAHAN  
All right, then. Wait till tomorrow.

#### SCENE II
(A part of the hotel lobby. BILL and JOE are seated in adjoining chairs.)

BILL  
That guy tried to make me kill myself today. He told me to go out and throw to the batters and put a lot o' stuff on the ball. You ought to have heard me bawl him. I bawled him good.

JOE  
Yeh, I heard ya.

### ACT III
(The sixth day.)

#### SCENE I
(The practice field. The players are engaged in a practice game.)

CALLAHAN  
Warm up, Lamewing. I'm going to work you the last two innings.

BILL  
I don't feel good, Jim.

CALLAHAN  
What's the matter? Your arm still sore?

BILL  
No, I don't feel good.

#### Scene II
(A part of the hotel lobby. BILL and JOE are seated in adjoining chairs.)

BILL  
Callahan tried to get me to go out there and pitch part o' that game today. And me with a rotten cold. He ought to of knew better. You ought to of heard me come back at him. I give it to him good.

JOE  
I'll bet you did.

### ACT IV
(The tenth day.)

#### SCENE I
(The ball park in San Francisco. It is just before game time.)

CALLAHAN  
Warm up, Lamewing. I'm going to start you. Take it easy.

LAMEWING  
I don't feel good, Jim.

CALLAHAN  
I didn't think you would You've been stuffing yourself in that dining room.

LAMEWING  
No, no, Jim. I feel good all over except my arm. I must o' strained it.

CALLAHAN  
What doing? Shoveling potatoes?

#### SCENE II
(A part of the Oakland hotel lobby. BILL and JOE are seated in adjoining chairs. It is evening.)

BILL  
You made a sucker o' yourself today. You went five innin's like as if it was mid-summer. I wouldn't kill myself for nobody.

JOE  
I didn't hurt myself. I feel good.

BILL  
Yes, you feel good now. But wait till along about April. Your arm'll be gone and they'll slip you to the sticks.

### ACT V
(The twentieth day.)

#### SCENE I
(The ball park at Los Angeles. It is just before game time.)

CALLAHAN  
Scott, you and Benz work this game. You start, Scotty. How do you feel today, Lamewing?

LAMEWING  
Me? I feel great.

CALLAHAN  
Wait a minute, Scotty. I guess I'll start Lamewing. Warm up, Lamewing.

LAMEWING  
I don't feel good, Jim.

#### SCENE II
(A part of the Los Angeles hotel lobby. It is evening. LAMEWING is seated in an easy chair. Enter CALLAHAN.)

CALLAHAN  
Lamewing, I've sold you to Tacoma. You didn't show me anything and I can't afford to carry you along. You can get a train out of here tonight. Here's your transportation. Good by. No hard feelings, I hope.

BILL  
No, no. That's all right, Jim. Good by, Jim.

(Exit CALLAHAN.)

#### SCENE III
(Another part of the lobby. JOE is standing within reaching distance of a cuspidor. Enter BILL)

BILL  
Just as I thought. That slob has canned me. I'm sold to Tacoma. And he didn't have the nerve to tell me so. He wrote me a note and then blowed out o' the hotel. I wish he'd told me to my face like a square guy. I'd of took a wallop at him.

JOE  
Sure.

BILL  
I got a rotten deal from this club. They didn't give me no chance.

JOE  
That's right. You wasn't worked at all, was ya?

BILL  
No, sir. I didn't pitch an innin'; never had a chance. Well, I'm goin' up to the room and pack up. I'll see ya when I come down. I hope Callahan gets back here before train time. I want to bust him one.

(Exit.)

#### SCENE IV
(A room in the hotel. It is late at night. Enter JOE.)

JOE  
Well, he's gone. (Goes over to the dresser.) I wonder wotinell I done with my comb. (Goes into the bathroom.) And my toothbrush, too. Wotinell kind o' hotel is this anyway?
