# Casual Encounters

(Being an faithful report, based on the recent statements of the principals, of the conversations that took place following the accidental meetings, in St. Louis and Chicago, of Ban Johnson, president of the American league, and Phil Ball, president of the St. Louis Feds.)

## Act 1

(Scene—Jim's Place, St. Louis)

PHIL  
Your face is familiar, but I can't recall the name.

BAN  
Johnson. Ban Johnson of Chicago.

PHIL  
O, yes; Dan Johnson.

BAN  
No. Ban, not Dan.

PHIL  
What business are you in, Mr. Johnson.

BAN  
I'm connected with the American baseball league; president of it.

PHIL  
Well, that's a coincidence. I'm connected with baseball, too. I'm Phil Ball, part owner of the Federal league club here.

BAN  
O, is that so? I've heard a great deal about the Federal league.

PHIL  
Yes, it's been given lots of newspaper space.

BAN  
Great place Jim's got here.

PHIL  
Wonderful. They say nobody can touch his maple nut sundaes.

BAN  
Why can't we try one?

PHIL  
We can (to waiter). Please bring up two of Jim's specials.

BAN  
Much dancing around St. Louis?

PHIL  
Yes, indeed. I'm at it every night.

BAN  
Are they still fox trotting?

PHIL  
Well, they call it that, but really it looks to me like a combination of the old two-step, polka, and gallop. They've cut out the trotting part.

BAN  
They have in Chicago, too.

PHIL  
Here's our sundaes.

BAN  
(tasting his'n) My, they certainly are good.

PHIL  
Grand!

BAN  
Heavenly!

PHIL  
I suppose you've had an early spring in Chicago, as we have had here.

BAN  
Yes, it's been beautiful weather so far; fine for baseball.

PHIL  
I think they ought to get baseball out of the courts and confine it to the ball field.

BAN  
I'm inclined to agree with you. Well, I must catch a train.

PHIL  
Sorry you have to hurry. Maybe we'll meet again some time.

BAN  
I hope so, I'm sure. Good-by, Mr. Hall.

PHIL  
Good-by, Mr. Thompson. Please to've metchu.

---

Scene—Fisher Building, Chicago.

BAN  
Well, Mr. Wall, what brings you away from St. Louis?

PHIL  
O, just here on a little business.

BAN  
How's St. Louis?

PHIL  
O, pretty dull. But I guess it's the same all over.

BAN  
Going to be here long?

PHIL  
No, I go back tonight. I'd take the noon train, only I made a date with a friend of mine here to play a little golf.

BAN  
O, you're a golfer, too, are you?

PHIL  
Yes, are you? 

BAN  
Just a beginner, you might say.

PHIL  
Well, I haven't been at it long, but I certainly do love it.

BAN  
There's no better diversion for a man who's generally shut up in an office.

PHIL  
Couldn't you join us this afternoon?

BAN  
No, I'm sorry, but I promised a friend of mine, Charley Comiskey, that I'd go out and see his baseball team play today. This is opening day. Very much like to have you go if you could get away from your golf.

PHIL  
I'd like to go, at that. My fingers are so blistered I can hardly hold a club.

BAN  
Well, say you call up your friend and say you've changed your mind.

PHIL  
I'll do that. I like to watch baseball.

BAN  
I do too. But I think they ought to get the game out of the courts and confine it to the field.

PHIL  
I'm inclined to agree with you.

## Act 3.
Scene—Comiskey Park.

BAN  
There's Charley Comiskey now.

PHIL  
Comiskey. That's a funny name. Polish?

BAN  
No, no. He's of Irish descent.

PHIL  
He's got a great plant here.

BAN  
Yes, and there's a great crowd here, too.

PHIL  
It would probably be even larger if there were no baseball war.

BAN  
That's true. I think they ought to get the game out of the courts and confine it to the field.

PHIL  
I'm inclined to agree with you.

BAN  
What do you say to an ice cream cone?
