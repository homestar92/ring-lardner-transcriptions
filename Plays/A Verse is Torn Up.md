(The Wake's Editor comes downtown to do his work, thinking there may be an idea in the mail. The mail contains two true bills, a note from the boss, a Niles paper, and a notice from the Press club. The ed. sits down at his desk.)

ED.  
(to himself) Well, it's got to be something and I suppose city series stuff is preferred. At that, I don't believe there's one-tenth the interest there was last year. There's an idea—a little verse to lead off with about the lack of interest in the series. Hiawatha style, so I won't have to bother with rhymes. No, I'll just make it a short one and rhyme it and then follow up with some bunk about—

(The phone rings.)

ED.  
Hello.

PHONE  
Sporting department?

Yes.

Sure?

ED.  
Sure.

PHONE  
Well, much obliged. We had a little argument and agreed to leave it to—

(ED. hangs up and returns to his desk.)

ED.  
Ought to get fans into the last line; ought to have it the last word. That means we'll need a rhyme for it in th esecond. Bans, Ban's, Ban Johnson's, Cans, Clans. Clans; that's it: the west and south side clans. Then there'll have to be first and third line rhymes. Ready for the fray. Fray's a cinch. Fray and today. Clans and fans. The stage is set, the men are fit and eager for the fray. That's all right.
