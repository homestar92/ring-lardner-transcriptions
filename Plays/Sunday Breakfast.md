## Characters
Le Père.  
La Mère.  
John, the eldest son.  
Jim, the middle-sized son.  
Bill, the son-of-a-gun.

---

JIM  
Why don't they have no school on Sunday?

LA MÈRE  
Any school.

BILL  
They have school at my school.

JIM  
O, your school! You don't have a school.

BILL  
I have a school, Mr. Jimmie.

JIM  
They wouldn't let you go to school; you're too little.

BILL  
I go to my school in a taxicab.

JIM  
Oh, boys don't go to school in taxicabs.

BILL  
They do in my school, Mr. Jimmie.

---

JOHN  
Say, keep still, kids, while I rhyme on one daddy:  
> Mr. Daddy was bigger than himself;  
> Thought he was bigger than anybody else  
> Went to a giant to measure his size,  
> But what to Mr. Daddy's surprise,  
> The giant was much more bigger.
>
> JIM  
Do all big mens stay home on Sunday?

LA MÈRE  
Nearly all.

BILL  
I always go downtown on Sunday.

JOHN  
Do you go down in your taxicab, Bill?

BILL  
No, Mr. John, I go down on the elevator.

JIM  
But why do you need to go down if there's no other big mens there?

BILL  
The downtown man is there. And they have a seat downtown that everybody can sit on all day.

---

JOHN  
What's the downtown man's name?

BILL  
The downtown man's name is Mr. Downtownman; that's his name, Mr. John.

JOHN  
Do you come home on the elevator, too?

BILL  
No, I come home in my automobile.

LE PÈRE  
What kind of an automobile have you got Mr. Bill?

BILL  
I've got a dangerous auto'bile. It runs over big ladies.

JOhn  
If you ran over ladies you'd get arrested.

BILL  
It runs over policemen, too.

JIM  
Say, the people upstairs must be God if they live above us.

LE PÈRE  
Yes, and I guess they think we're—

LA MÈRE  
Ssh!
