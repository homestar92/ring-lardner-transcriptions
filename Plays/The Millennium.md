(President Gilmore of the Federal league is looking forward to the day when a baseball magnate can tell the truth.)

## PERSONS REPRESENTED
George Washington, President of the U. S. League  
Bill Neverly, President of the Universal League  
Sam, Reporter for the Times  
Harold, Reporter for the Mail

### SCENE 1.

(Headquarters of the U.S. league, 4 p.m. SAM and HAROLD enter and find GEORGE paring his fingernails.)

GEORGE  
Hello, pests.

SAM and HAROLD  
Hello.

GEORGE  
I have some cigars here that cost 10 cents apiece, and I suppose I've got to give up a couple of them

SAM  
Thanks.

HAROLD  
Any news today?

GEORGE  
I don't know what news is, but I'd like to get my name in the papers if you can fix for it.

SAM  
There's a story going round to the effect that you fellas are on your uppers.

GEORGE  
Well, not all of us. The whole league lost money this season, but one or two of the clubs are owned by men who can stand it. The others, though, are in awful shape and wish they'd never heard of a baseball.

SAM  
Is it true that you're looking for peace?

GEORGE  
You bet it is. But we have to go about it carefully and not seem over-anxious or they might realize what we're up against and tell us to jump in the lake.

HAROLD  
Is there any truth in the story about Ryan of your league trying to buy the Lowlanders?

GEORGE  
Search me. He never tells me anything.

SAM  
Well, so long.

GEORGE  
So long. Be sure to give me a little the best of it.

(SAM and HAROLD go out.)

### SCENE 2.

(Headquarters of the Universal league. 4:30 p.m. SAM and HAROLD enter and find BILL polishing his shoes with a handkerchief.)

BILL  
Good afternoon, bores. Have a cigar.

SAM and HAROLD  
Thanks.

BILL  
I knew you wouldn't refuse, you poor grafters.

SAM  
Any news today?

BILL  
Well, there is some, but it makes us look bad so I don't like to cough it up.

HAROLD  
What is it?

BILL  
Well, we've had a secret meeting and decided that we can't go on bucking the U. S. league, so we've got to start overtures for peace.

SAM  
Did your league lose money this season?

BILL  
Sure, and that isn't the worst. These bum have fixed it so we'll have to pay outrageous salaries to ball players next year, and even then we won't know whether we can hang on to them or not. But there's one cinch—unless we have some sort of working agreement with the U. S. we'll lose our shirt. Most of our club owners are about ready to give up.

HAROLD  
Is there any truth in the story about Ryan trying to buy the Lowlanders?

BILL  
I don't know. I figure that the story was given out so he'd get to thinking about it and possibly make an offer. We'd be tickled to death if he would.

SAM  
Will your club owners follow any course mapped out by you?

BILL  
How can I tell what desperate men will do? I'm tired of talking to you boys now and I wish you'd beat it. Try to give our side the best of it in the papers.

SAM and HAROLD  
So long.

(SAM and HAROLD go out.)

### SCENE 3.

(A downtown street. 5 p.m. SAM and HAROLD enter and walk along slowly.)

SAM  
Well, what are we going to print?

HAROLD  
Why not print what they gave us?

SAM  
Are you crazy? When the boss read it, he'd can us.

HAROLD  
What for?

SAM  
For being lit up on duty.

HAROLD  
I guess you're right. Let's fire Hank O'Day.
