# The Fe-Mail Carrier

(She rings the Browns' doorbell. Mrs. Brown opens the door.)

MRS. BROWN  
Oh, good morning, dearie. Aren't you nearly frozen?

SHE  
I'm getting so I don't mind it. But I bet my nose is a sight.

MRS. B.  
It doesn't look bad. Can't you come in?

SHE  
Not possibly. I'm terribly behind.

(She enters the house and looks in the hall mirror.)

MRS. B.  
I don't suppose there's any mail for me.

SHE  
(searches mail pouch and at length produces a powder puff, which she uses.) I'll see in just a minute. (She finishes powdering, replaces the puff and draws out a bundle of letters. She looks through them.) Yes, here's something. But I'm afraid it's just a bill. (She examines it.) It's from the Economy Dry Goods, on Elston. (She hands the letter to Mrs. Brown.)

MRS. B.
(examines both sides of an envelope) Why, I haven't boughten anything there for weeks and weeks. (They enter the living room and sit on the couch.) This must be some mistake.

---

SHE  
It probably is. Some of these stores are terrible. I don't believe they keep any books. Why, way last May I—

MRS. B.  
I can't imagine what this can be fore. I know I paid cash for that yarn.

SHE  
Maybe it isn't a bill. Maybe it's just a circular.

(Mrs. Brown opens the envelope and takes out some advertising matter.)

MRS. B.  
Why, sure enough! It's just a circular. I was positive I didn't owe anything.

SHE  
(glancing through a bundle of letters) Is it Mrs. E. C. Hayes that lives next door?

MRS. B.  
Yes. They moved in a week ago. I've only seen her once or twice, only once to speak to. She seems pleasant enough, but her clothes are a mess.

SHE  
(examining a letter) This is for her. It's from Danville.

MRS. B.  
That's funny. I understood her to say she came from Wisconsin. But maybe I'm mistaken. No, I'm sure she said Wisconsin.

SHE  
Well, anyway, this is postmarked Danville. It's a woman's writing.

MRS. B.  
(leans over and looks at the letter) Sure enough, it is. Danville, Illinois. Maybe she has relatives there.

SHE  
Or it may be her husband's relations.

MRS. B.  
She told me where her husband's home is, but I forget.

SHE  
She may have a married sister in Danville.

MRS. B.  
She didn't say anything about her.

---

SHE  
(looking at another letter) And here's one from Rockford for Mrs. Robert Cain.

MRS. B.  
Oh, yes, her son, Tom, is at Camp Grant. He writes Bertha once a week.

SHE  
Isn't the war awful?

MRS. B.  
Simply terrible!

SHE  
(rising) Well, I can't stay forever. I'm way behind now.

MRS. B.  
Oh, before you go, I want to show you what I'm knitting. It's a sweater.

SHE  
(sitting down again) Oh, are you? I am, too. Look! (She digs into mail pouch and produces her knitting.)

MRS. B.  
Oh! Why it's almost exactly the same color as mine. Wait till I get it.

(She waits.)
