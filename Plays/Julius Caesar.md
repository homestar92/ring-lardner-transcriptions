## Scene 2—The same. A Room in Cæsar's Palace.

(Thunder and lightning. Enter Cæsar in his nightshirt.)

CÆS.  
> A person has a lovely chance to sleep  
> In this here dump, with thunder raising he⸺ll  
> Outdoors and the old lady having nightmares.  
> Three different times Calphurnia's hollered out:  
> "They've croaked my man!"... Now, who the he⸺ll is this?

(Enter a servant.)

SERV.  
> Was they anything you wanted, Marse Julius?

CÆS.  
> Go to the 'phone and call up Madame Rose.  
> Ask if it's safe for me to leave this joint.

SERV.  
> Yessah. (Exits.)

(Enter Calphurnia.)

CAL.  
> Julius, what do you mean by getting up  
> This early! You ain't going to work today.

CÆS.  
> I'll do as I dam please.

CAL.  
>     But listen, dearie:  
> You know darn well that I'm not superstitious,  
> But they's so many thing's that's came off lately.  
> For instance, yesterday, at Brutus' flat,  
> Some boob put up his bumbershoot indoors;  
> And Friday night, while we was driving home,  
> A black cat run acrost the boulevard  
> In front of us; and down on State last week  
> I walked under a window washer's ladder;  
> And yesterday, while strolling with the pup,  
> I seen a pin and didn't pick it up.

CÆS.  
> Oh, go to bed and throw another nightmare!  
> If I  am going to croak, I'm going to croak,  
> So you should worry.  
(Reënter Servant.)
>    Well, George, what's the dope!

SERV.  
> Rose say yo'-all had better stick to home.  
> She rolled dem bones out and out come two big sixes

CÆS.  
> Say, listen, I am hep to Rosie's dice;  
> If she wants two big sixes, out they come.  
> I won't stay home on no such hunch as that.  
> I ain't no sucker.

CAL.  
>     No one said you was,  
> But, dearie, stay at home to please your frau.  
> We'll simply 'phone to Mr. Antony  
> And ask him to tell the senators  
> That you have got the flu. Once in your life  
> You might pay some attention to your wife.

CÆS.  
> All right, I'll stay this once if you'll shut up;  
> But it  is just because if I did go  
> And have bad luck, you'd say: "I told you so."  
(Enter Decius.)  
> Here's Decius. We'll give him my alibi.

DEC.  
> Good-morning, Jul. How's every little thing?  
> My fliv's outside to take you to the senate.

CÆS.  
> I'm mighty glad that you dropped in, old scout.  
> I'll send a message by you to the boys  
> To tell them that I won't be there today.

CAL.  
> He's sicker than a dog.

CÆS.  
>     Oh, can that junk!  
> Just simply say I won't be on the job.

DEC.  
> But tell me why, for if I give the boys  
> No reason, they'll all laugh and say you're stewed.

CÆS.  
> Well, then, just simply tell them I won't come.  
> Because the Missus here wants me at home.

DEC.  
> They'd bust their sides if I should tell them that.  
> They know how henpecked Cæsar is—like he⸺ll!  
> But listen, there is something coming off  
> This morning that you wouldn't want to miss.  
> We're going to cut the Senate session short,  
> Then go to Casca's flat and play some stud.

CÆS.  
> That's me! Calphurnia, fetch my buds  
> And pants. And may your nightmares bring me luck!

DEC.  
> Yatta boy, Jul!

CAL.  
>   I hope you lose your shirt!
