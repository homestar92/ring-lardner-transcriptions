## A Friend of Frank's

### Characters
Frank M. Schulte, an Outfielder.  
Heine Zimmerman, a Bear Wherever You Put Him
Si Simpson, a Recruit
George Pest, a Cub Fan.
Joe Bug, another Cub Fan

Scene—Old Chi and Tampa, Fla. Time—March

### SCENE I
(A ginmill in old Chi. PEST and BUG stand with their feet on the rail.)

BUG  
Some cold tonight.

PEST  
Some cold is right. I'll be glad to get away from here.

BUG  
Goin' away?

PEST  
Yes, I'm beatin' it to Florida tonight. Goin' to drop in on the Cubs at Tampa.

BUG  
Gosh, I wisht I could go along. Know any o' the Cubs personally?

PEST  
Sure! I got a lot o' pals on the team. Johnny Evers and Frank Chance was two o' my best friends. Course they're both of 'em gone now, but I know Tom Needham and Larry Cheney and Roger Bresnahan and Heine Zimmerman and Frank Schulte—

BUG  
Do you know Schulte?

PEST  
Do I know Schulte? Say, him and I is just like brothers. I call him Wildfire.

BUG  
Does he know you're comin'?

PEST  
No, I'm fixin' to surprise him.

BUG  
I'll bet he'll be glad to see you.

PEST  
Will he be glad to see me? Say, he'll pretty near kiss me. I can have anything he's got.

BUG  
Well, I hope you have a good time. Good luck and please to of met you.

PEST  
Same to you. So long, and maybe I'll run acrost you when I get back to dear old Chi.

### SCENE II

(The lobby of the hotel at Tampa. The athletes are lolling around. Enter PEST. He approaches SCHULTE.)

PEST  
Are you one o' the Cubs?

SCHULTE  
Me? No. I work for one o' the Chicago papers.

PEST  
O, is that so? *Tribune*?

SCHULTE  
No, I'm Mr. Fish of the Journal

PEST  
Pesase to meet you Mr. Fish. Is Schulte and Zimmerman anywheres around?

SCHULTE  
I think they have gone to church. No. Here comes Schulte now.

(Enter ZIMMERMAN)

PEST  
He looks bigger in his citizen's clo'es

SCHULTE  
I didn't get your name. Pest? Mr. Pest, shake hands with Mr. Schulte.

(PEST and ZIMMERMAN shake hands.)

PEST  
Please to meet you Mr. Schulte. I've often watched you play. I've often wanted to meet you. I wanted to ask you how you hit them home runs.

ZIM  
O, I'm some hitter all right. But I wisht I could hit 'em like Zimmerman.

PEST  
I think you're a better hitter than Zimmerman. He strikes out a good deal.

ZIM  
Yes, but he does it on purpose If he didn't strike out oncet in awhile no pitchers wouldn't pitch agin him.

PEST  
What was the matter with him last year?

ZIM  
O, him and Evers didn't get along. He knowed so much more about managin' a ball club that Evers got jealous.

PEST  
Any truth in the story about him and Evvers havin' a fight?

ZIM  
Sure they was. Zimmerman give him an awful beatin' Then Cheney and Bresnahan and Needham and Archer cut in, and Zimmerman licked all of 'em. Then they called in four or five policemen and Zimmerman kicked 'em all out the door.

PEST  
I'd like to meet this guy Zimmerman.

ZIM  
You better keep away from him when he gets mad.

SCHULTE  
There he comes now. I'll interduce you.

(SI SIMPSON enters and is hailed by SCHULTE.)

SIMPSON  
Whaddaya want? 

SCHULTE  
Mr. Zimmerman, shake hands with Mr. ⸻. I didn't get your name? Pest? Shake hands with Mr. Pest.

(SIMPSON and PEST shake hands.)

PEST  
Please to meet you, Mr. Zimmerman. I've often saw you play.

SIMPSON  
What's the gag?

ZIMMERMAN  
Gag? Where do you get that stuff. Heine?

SIMPSON  
Tryin' to kid somebody?

(He passes on.)

PEST  
What's th ematter with him?

SCHULTE  
HE's always like that He's got the swell head.

PEST  
Is he sore at me?

SCHULTE  
O, Heine's sore at everybody.

PEST  
I've always heard he was a peculiar guy, but I never knowed he was so bad as that. You could tell by the way he acts on the field that he's swell headed.

ZIM  
He ain't no more swell headed than some o' them bugs up in the stand nor bone headed, neither.

PEST  
What's the amtter, Mr. Schulte? You talk like you was sore at them bugs.

ZIM  
You bet I'm sore at 'em, and if you're one of 'em you'd better beat it before you get hurt, you bum.

(Exit ZIMMERMAN)

PEST  
Gee! He got pretty sore, didn't he? I always heart that Schulte was a good natured, quiet cuss.

SCHULTE  
Schulte? You never can tell nothin' about him. You know he has epileptic fits, and he's just as li'ble to shoot you as look at you. If I was you I'd beat it, or he might jump you when he comes back.

PEST  
Well, let's you and I go somewheres and get a drink.

SCHULTE  
I'd like to go with you but I've got to write some stuff for my paper. I have to wire in ten thousand words yet tonight.

PEST  
Well, I may see you later. When you see Schulte tell him I didn't mean nothin', and I'm sorry he got sore at me. I'm beatin' it back to old Chi tomorrow. I expected to be here a week but they wired me to hurry home. So long, Mr. Fish.

SCHULTE  
So long, Mr. ⸻. I didn't get your name. Anyway, so long.

### SCENE III

(A cigar store in old Chi. BUG is leaning against the counter. Enter PEST.)

BUG  
Well, are you back in old Chi?

PEST  
Yes, I'm here again. Had a great time, and seen all they was to see.

BUG  
Did you get to Tampa?

PEST  
Sure I was there a week. I had some time with the Cubs. Say, but that's a nice bunch o' fellahs.

BUG  
See your friend Schulte?

PEST  
Sure. Him and I was out together pretty near every night.

BUG  
Did you meet any o' the rest o' the boys?

PEST  
O, I knowed 'em all except the recruits before I went down there. We all went out together pretty near every night and lifted a few. They're good fellahs. They ain't a swell headed one in the gang.
