# Follies of 1917

## SCENE 1.
(Music Room of Pat Moran's home)

Song ..................Pat Moran

> There's a Baker down  
> In Quakertown  
>    Who needed dough, 'twould seem  
> He has it now,  
> But Holy Cow!
>    I ain't got no team!

## SCENE 2.
(Third base at Comiskey Park. An October afternoon. Scenery by Urban Faber.)

HERR ZIMMERMAN  
"Mr. Weaver, there's some one calling on you."

MR. WEAVER  
"Who is it?"

HERR ZIMMERMAN  
"His name is Faber."

MR. WEAVER  
"Tell him I'm not home yet."

## SCENE 3.
(Music room in the home of Cornelius McGillicuddy.)

Song ..................Connie Mack

> I do not know who'll land on top;
> I do not care a rip.
> I only know I'm sure to cop
> The seller championship.

## SCENE 4.
(Infield at the Polo Grounds.)

HERR ZIMMERMAN, having popped the side out again, walks toward his position  
"You ain't showed me nothin', Sy-cot."

## SCENE 5.
(Comiskey Park. A Saturday afternoon in October.)

Song ..................Chorus of White Sox

> Of all the men New York has got
>     There's none as sweet as Sallee.
> The stuff he's got's the stuff we swat,
>     For he pitches in our alley.

## SCENE 6.
(Bedroom in a Texas hotel.)

HERR HERZOG  
"Kamerad!"

## SCENE 7.
(Office at the American league park, Philadelphia.)

MR. SHIBE  
"I had an awful nightmare last night. I dreamed we had Ty Cobb."

STENOGRAPHER  
"Nightmare!"

MR. SHIBE  
"Yes. The understanding was we had to keep him."

## SCENE 8.
(Polo Grounds. A Monday afternoon in October.)

COLLINS  
(at third base) "I can beat you running, Heine."

ZIM  
"Yes, you can!"

COLLINS  
"I'll say I can"

(They start.)

COLLINS  
(looking round) "I'm a faster man than you are, Heine Zim."

(This line is copped from Friend Tad.)

ZIM  
"Yes, you are!"

COLLINS  
"I'll say so!"

RARIDEN  
"Throw the ball."

ZIM  
"Who'll I throw it to? Klem?"

HOLKE  
(at first base) "This is a mighty exciting game. I'm glad I could get to see it."

## SCENE 9.

(A street in Pittsburgh)

FIRST CITIZEN  
"I see that Barney's arranging for the club to play exhibition games at the army camps."

SECOND CITIZEN  
"Well, by spring the soldiers'll be tough enough to stand it."
