# La Maledizione Di Pudelaggio
(Yuletide Opera in Two Acts.)

## Characters.
Il Turkey (tenor).  
Il Plum Pudding (basso).  
Lo Gravy (liquid barytone).  
Gen. Nutpick (basso).  
Lo Cranberry Sauce (falsetto).  
La Mashed Potato (contralto).  
La Yam (soprano).  
Also Dinner Plates, Knives, Forks, Spoons, Napkins, Bibs, Olives, Celery, Giblets, Salt and Pepper, Nuts and Raisins, etc.

## Atto I.
Scene—The dinner table. Christmas day at 1:30 p. m.

(At the rise of the curtain, the stage is set for dinner, with theKnives, Forks, Spoons, Napkins, and Bibs on the scene.)

KNIVES  
Oh, we are the knives.

FORKS  
And we are their wives.

SPOONS  
And we are the spoons indispensable.

KNIVES  
We're slender young blades.

FORKS  
We're shapely young maids.

SPOONS  
We're homely, but awfully sensible.

KINVES, FORKS, AND SPOONS  
> Though we're all nice and clean  
> At the start of the scene  
>   Having just had a Swedish massage,  
> At the end of the meal,  
> We'll look how we feel  
>   Like the prop. of a one-horse garage.

CHORUS OF NAPKINS  
> Oh, we are the napkins  
> To lay in the lapkins  
>   Or tuck in the V's of the vests.
> Just now we seem dressed up  
> But ugh! we'll be messed up  
> By Mr. and Mrs. and Guests.

CHORUS OF BIBS  
> Oh, we are the bibs,  
> Supposed to catch dribs  
>   From the wavering spoons of the brats.  
> Just now we look white,  
> But we'll soon be a sight,  
>   What with sugar and starches and fats.

---

(Enter the Celery and Olives.)

CHORUS OF OLIVES  
We're olives from the tepid states.

CHORUS OF CELERY  
We're celery from Michigan.

CHORUS OF OLIVES  
We'd like to live in sep'rate plates.

CHORUS OF CELERY  
But we're in this same dish again.

CHORUS OF CELERY AND OLIVES  
> To park us together is hellish,  
> But who cares what the relishes relish!

(Enter a Pile of Dinner Plates.)

CHORUS OF DINNER PLATES  
> Dinner Plates of chiny,  
> All so white an shiny,  
> When fair Hannah brings us in,  
> Means the meal will soon begin.  
> Knives and FOrks, stand steady!  
> Relishes, be ready!  
> Bibs and Napkins, cease your din!  
> Il Turkey's coming in!

---

TUTTI  
> Christmas dinner's ready!  
> Everyone stand steady!  
> Stop this noise and clatter!  
> See! The royal platter!

(Turkey enters on a platter attended by a guard of Giblets and followed at a little distance by Mashed Potato and the beautiful Yam.)

TUTTI  
Hail the festive bird!

LA MASHED POTATO  
(aside) Soon to be interred.

(Turkey takes the place of honor at the head of the table. Gravy enters silently and oozes up to him.)

LO GRAVY  
Sire!

IL TURKEY  
Well, Thickness?

LO GRAVY  
I come to warn thee. Plum Pudding loves the fair Yam, whom thou hast stolen. As we left the kitchen, I heard him say, "That bird will get the stuffing knocked out of him!"

IL TURKEY  
Chestnuts! (Beckons to the fair Yam.) My candied Kid!

LA YAM  
My Lord!

IL TURKEY  
Why dost thou so tremble?

---

LA YAM  
(Aria.)  
> My loved one, trouble is brewing;  
> Our love  will be our undoing.  
> I just got a good long flash  
> At White Potato, thy former mash.  
> She loves the still. Beware  
> The curse of the pomme de terre!

IL TURKEY  
> What do I care a damn  
> So long as I have thee, Yam?

LA YAM  
> She swears, that as she is mashed,  
> She'll live to see thee slashed.

(Enter Cranberry Sauce, who rushes up to Turkey.)

LO CRANBERRY SAUCE  
> With brandy on his breath,  
> Plum Pudding plots thy death.  
> Thy human enemy  
> Shall lay thee low, saith he.

---

IL TURKEY
> (defiantly) Go ride a cock hoss  
> To Banbury Cross,  
> Old Cranberry Sauce!

LO CRANBERRY SAUCE  
I have warned thee!

(A carving fork is suddenly jammed into Turkey's carcass and a huge stiletto pinks his flesh.)

IL TURKEY  
Mio Dio!

## Atto II.
Scene—The same dinner table, forty minutes later.

(The curtain's rise discloses Il Turkey, Lo Gravy, Lo Cranberry Sauce, La Mashed Potato and La Yam still on the scene, but in a horribly mangled condition; also Knives, Forks, Napkins, Bibs, etc., all showing signs of the severs ordeal they have gone through. The Salt and Pepper Shakers ballet appears in "The Dance of the Seasons." As the dance ends, the utensils and naperies limp to the front of the scene.)

CHORUS OF NAPKINS  
> Oh, we are the napkins  
> That shielded the lapkins  
>   And eke an occasional vest.  
> We're soiled, but by gosh!  
> 'Twill come out in the wash,  
>   And we'll all take another year's rest.  

CHORUS OF BIBS  
> And we are the bibs,  
> Supposed to catch dribs  
>   From the wavering spoons of the young.  
> We've missed enough gravy  
> To float England's navy,  
>   Yet see what a lot of it clung!

KNIVES, FORKS, AND SPOONS  
> We were awfully thin  
> When this opera begin,  
>   Now you hardly would know us at all.  
> We've took on so much weight,  
> Which is strange to relate,  
>   When you think of the things we let fall.

---

IL TURKEY  
> (Feebly) Where are my giblets?

LO CRANBERRY SAUCE  
> On the kids' biblets.

IL TURKEY  
> And where art thou, my Yam?

LA YAM  
> (Weakly) Here, wretched bird, I am,  
> Killed by my rival's curse.

LA MASHED POTATO  
> Thou liest!

IL TURKEY  
>   Good-night, nurse!  
> Thou'rt strong and well, false Tube?

LA MASHED POTATO  
> As weak as thou, old boob!  
> And all thy fault, old Turk!  
> Plum Pudding's curses work  
> I heard him loud thee damn,  
> When thou ran'st off with Yam.

IL TURKEY  
> I'll be avenged on Plum;  
> I'll put him on the bum!

---
(Il Turkey, La Mashed Potato, La Yam, and attendants stagger out, followed by Dinner Plates, Knives, Forks, etc. At the door they meet and pass a chorus of Nuts and Raisnis, who enter boldly.)

A NUT  
Didst thou see?

A RAISIN  
No; I'm seedless.

A NUT  
Yes, and at this stage needless.

A RAISIN  
Lay off me, Nut. What was there to see?

A NUT  
Il Turkey, La Mashed POtato, and La Yam, all but dead.

A RAISIN  
Ah! Drunken Plum Pudding's curse.

A NUT  
Hush! Here he comes!

(Il Plum Pudding enters, all lit up like a busy sector.)

IL PUDDING  
> I've lost my Yam, but what the he⸺ll!  
> She's lost to Mr. Turk, as well.  
> No more that red-necked bird will try 
> Pudding it over on this guy.

(Gen. Nutpick enters hurriedly, rushing up to Il Pudding.)

GEN. NUTPICK  
> Sire, thy curse has partly flivveth;  
> Fair Yam is dead, but Turkey liveth.

A NUT  
Turkey livers?

IL PUDDING  
(Furiously, to the Nut) Fermez la bouche, Nut! (To Gen. Nutpick) What's this you say? Turkey's alive?

GEN. NUTPICK  
Si, and says he'll be stewed tomorrow.

IL PUDDIN  
Mio Dio!

(End of the opera.)
