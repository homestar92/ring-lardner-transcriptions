# Fish.
## Play in Seven Scenes

### Characters
Smith, a Person.  
Mrs. Smith, his wife.  
Jones, a Friend.  
Cole, a Barber.  
Page, a Waiter.  
Brown, a Friend

### SCENE 1.

Breakfast room in the Smiths' house. 7:30 A. M.

MRS. SMITH  
(looking up from newspaper) It says fair and warmer tomorrow.—It says there's almost sure to be a revolution in Germany.—It says Austria wants to quit.—It says the Allies gained almost four miles and took 60,000 prisoners.—It says Russia is against a separate peace.

SMITH  
(getting up) I've got to hurry. (Goes to her and kisses her fondly).

MRS. SMITH  
Don't work too hard! What do you want fo dinner?

SMITH  
Oh, anything. Fish, I guess. Good-bye sweetheart.

(He goes to the train.)

### SCENE 2.

Smoker of the 7:48

JONES  
The Kaiser's licked. He can't get any more money and he knows he's got to carry Austria and Turkey as well as himself. And there's tuberculosis and smallpox and everything else in the German army. And while the people aren't actually dying of starvation, their food is so inadequate that it's affecting the fiber of the race. He'll have to quit.

SMITH  
Light a fresh one, Jones.

(Hands Jones a good cigar.)

### SCENE 3.

A barber shop. 11:30 A. M.

COLE  
I see where all the munitions factories in Germany are going to strike. That'll just about end it.—I see where the Kaiser's going to ask for peace. It looks like the real stuff this time.—It'll be over before we're in it.

SMITH  
Yeah, I'll have a massage, too.

### SCENE 4.

A cafe. 12:50 P. M.

SMITH  
Bring me another cup of coffee.

PAGE  
I see where they sunk sixty British ships in a week.—England's up against it. They can either give up or starve.—And the French are all in and ready to call it off.—There don't seem to be no way o' fightin' the dam things.—First thing you know, the British navy'll get blowed to pieces and then where are we at?

SMITH  
How much is that check?

PAGE  
Eighty cents.

(Smith lays a half dollar, a quarter, and a nickel on the table and exits.)

### SCENE 5.

Smoker on the 5:15.

BROWN  
We're just getting in at the wrong time. Germany's got a new crop coming and they can take care of themselves another year. But we haven't got enough for ourselves, let alone England and France, and besides, we can't et anything over to them. They're just about through. Pretty soon we'll be fighting by ourselves.—France's troops are exhausted and England can't send any more. They've got to keep 'em at home to guard against an invasion.—It looks as if the old son-of-a-gun had put it over. We're backing a loser.—Will you have a cigarette?

SMITH  
I'm off tobacco. It makes me logey.

(The train reaches Smith's town.)

BROWN  
Well, good-night.

(Smith does not reply.)

### SCENE 6.

Living-room in Smith's home.

MRS. SMITH  
Did you bring home an evening paper?

SMITH  
(gruffly) It's in my overcoat.

MRS. SMITH  
Did you have a hard day?

(Smith does not reply.)

MRS. SMITH  
Dinner's on the table.

(Smith goes to the dining-room. Mrs. Smith gets the evening paper from his overcoat pocket and follows him.)

MRS. SMITH  
(looking up from the paper) Rainy and cold tomorrow.

SMITH  
What the hell! Are we going to have fish for dinner every night?

(He kicks viciously at the table leg and hurts his toe.)
