## CHARACTERS.
Le Père.  
La Mère.  
John, the eldest son.  
Jim, the middle-sized son.  
Bill, the son-of-a-gun  

JIM  
Daddy, did you ever see a mole?

JOHN  
I seen a dead mole once over in Niles.

LA MÈRE  
Saw a dead mole.

JIM  
I mean a live mole. Did you?

LE PÈRE  
Lots of them.

BILL  
I see live moles every dya.

JOHN  
Where, Bill?

BILL  
In my room.

JIM  
Oh, live moles in your room! What do they do in there?

BILL  
THey eat the big mens, that's what they do.

JIM  
Oh, moles eat big mens yourself!

BILL  
They do eat big mens, Mr. Jimmie; that's what moles do.

JIM  
Oh, they might eat Teeny Weenies, but they couldn't eat big mens bees they're too little.

JOHN  
Here is a rhyme for you:  
> Mr. Sober Sides was hungry and dry.  
> But all he could see was a team passing by.

LE PÈRE  
Who is Mr. Sober Sides?

JOHN  
He is a cousin to the Kaiser and a grand-cousin to the Crown Prince. And here is another rhyme and it's called "About the Madmen That Went Into the Woods."

LE PÈRE  
Go ahead.

JOHN  
> Once there was a madman that went into the wood  
> To cut down trees to make a fire if he could.  
>   But instead he cut his arm,  
>   And he made a great alarm  
> That deafened everybody in the wood.

LE PÈRE  
That's fine. It's too bad Jim can't rhyme like that.

JIM  
I can too rhyme. Here is my rhyme:  
> Mr. Rabbit came out of his house underground  
> And went into the pond.

JOHN  
How about you, Bill? Can you rhyme one?

BILL  
What are we going to have for dessert?

JIM  
Oh, dessert yourself! We don't have dessert bees it's breakfast.

BILL  
I always have dessert; that's what I always have. And right after dessert I'm going down and tell the janitor to stop shoveling the slippery. And if he doesn't stop it right this minute, I'll kick him in the mouth.

LA MÈRE  
That isn't a nice way to talk.

BILL  
That's the way I talk, Mrs. Man.
