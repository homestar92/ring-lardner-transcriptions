## Characters.
William Gleason, manager of the White Sox.  
James Burke, manager of the Browns.  
Scene—American league ball park in St. Louis.  
Time—2 p. m. Friday

GLEASON  
Well, Jim, your club looked great yesterday. They reminded me like a college team we played down in Texas. Only the college boys could think.

BURKE  
Well, your club won't look so good when they go agin some pitchin'.

GLEASON  
Well, me club will look good as long as we're here, then.

BURKE  
Well, this bird I'm pitchin' today'll make a fool out of you.

GLEASON  
Well, he'll have to be good to win for that club you've got.

BURKE  
Well, I'm satisfied with my club.

GLEASON  
Well, then, they's nothin' more to be said. Only I kind of figured maybe you wasn't so easily satisfied and I was goin' to say I could help your club. I could make a ball club out of your club. IYour club would be right up there fightin' if you'd leave me give you some help.

BURKE  
What's your idear?

GLEASON  
Well, it looks to me like you need some strength in the outfield and your infield and maybe you could use some pitchers and maybe a couple catches.

BURKE  
Well?

GLEASON  
Well, you've got one left-hand first baseman, but I'll give you another left-hand first-baseman and another infielder and one outfielder or two outfielders and three pitchers and that would just about make your club.

BURKE  
Well, spill it.

GLEASON  
Well, that's seven or eight men, say, that I'm givin' away and that's a good many men to be givin' away. But I've got a good club meself and don't need no help, but I'd like to do you a favor for old times. So I'd be willin' to give you the eight men for nothin' as you haven't nobody I want meself. Bud I'd have to get one man to make it look right to the fans up home. But still, you haven't nobody I want meself.

BURKE  
Well?

GLEASON  
Well, I'll give you the eight men and you give me one man and—well, lemme see—who shall it be? Well, say Sisler.

BURKE  
I'm goin in and get a sweater.
