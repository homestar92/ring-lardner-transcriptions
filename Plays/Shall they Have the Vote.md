# Shall They Have the Vote?

## SCENE 1.
(Monday p.m. Lobby of Auditorium theater. Crowd awaiting turns to buy tickets.)

LADY  
(at head of line) What is the opera Saturday?

BOX OFFICE MAN  
Afternoon or evening?

LADY  
Afternoon

B. O. Man  
"Romeo and Juliet."

LADY  
With Muratore and Galli-Serky?

B. O. Man  
Yes, it's a repetition.

LADY  
The same as last Friday night?

B. O. Man  
Yes.

LADY  
What are the prices?

B. O. Man  
Five dollars for lower floor.

LADY  
I wanted two—my sister's coming with me.

B. O. Man  
We only have singles.

LADY  
I wouldn't want singles.

B. O. Man  
I'm sorry.

LADY  
You say you have singles?

B. O. Man  
Yes; here's one in the fourteenth row.

LADY  
Is that the best you have?

B. O. Man  
Yes.

LADY  
Well, I'd have to have two together.

B. O. Man  
I'm sorry.

LADY  
(going out) My sister's coming with me.

## SCENE 2.
(Monday p.m. A canned music shop. Crowd awaiting attention.)

LADY  
I wonder if you have any Kreisler records.

SALESMAN  
Oh, yes.

LADY  
I wonder if you have that little thing he played last time. It was an encore. It began with an S.

SALESMAN  
Perhaps it's in the catalog. Just look under Kreisler.

LADY  
Would you kindly look for me? I'd have to put on my glasses. It began with an S.

(SALESMAN opens catalog and reads the list of Kreisler records.)

LADY  
No; it's none of those. It was just a little thing. He played it for an encore. I'm positive it began with an S, but I may be mistaken.

SALESMAN  
I'm afraid we can't find it unless you have the name of it.

LADY  
It was a pretty little thing; sort of simple. He plays so many things that really have no melody. This was an encore. It began with S.

SALESMAN  
I don't think we have it.

LADY  
Do you know another store where I'd be likely to find it?

SALESMAN  
I'm afraid not.

LADY  
Well, I'll have to try somewhere else.

(She goes out.)
